# Reference evidence: Best SaaS Product Demo Video | Teamble

Original document: mori-launch/CREATIVE_DIRECTION.md
Reference: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xNUx-rMGvvw


The reference is a 98-second animated product demo with no live footage and no
photography. Everything on screen is either **typography** or a **fragment of
product UI** floating on a lit gradient stage. There is no narrator subtitle
burned in; the story is carried by on-screen copy, interface sound, and a
music bed.

### 1.2 Motion language (the twelve rules)

1. **Stage inversion is the punctuation.** The film alternates near-black and
   near-white stages, cut hard on the beat. Every act change is an inversion.
   Dark carries the brand and the claim; light carries the product and the proof.
2. **Word-append typography.** Lines build one word at a time. The whole line
   re-centres and *auto-scales down* as it grows, so the last word always lands
   at a readable size. Each word owns a colour.
3. **Type-on with a caret.** Openers literally type, with a blinking caret and
   an underline stroke that morphs into a pill container.
4. **Blooms, not backgrounds.** Every frame is lit by two or three enormous,
   soft, slowly-drifting colour blobs. On black they are crescents pinned to a
   frame edge; on white they are full-frame washes. Nothing is evenly lit.
5. **UI fragments, not screenshots.** The film almost never shows a whole app
   screen. It shows *one card, one pill, one row, one button*, isolated on the
   stage with a soft drop shadow. This is what keeps 98 seconds legible.
6. **Cards that build themselves.** A card grows a row at a time, each row
   springing in and pushing the card's height. Numbers tick with a counter.
7. **The cursor presses real UI.** A chunky hand cursor arrives *before* the
   press, dips on the click, and a soft ring pulses out. Every press changes
   visible state — no decorative clicking.
8. **Perspective slabs.** Documents and dashboards are thin physical slabs
   (CSS `perspective` + `rotateX`/`rotateY`), always counter-rotating toward
   flat while the camera pushes in. Never a literal 3D environment.
9. **The dive is the transition.** To move from a slab to a detail, the camera
   scales and crops into one control instead of cutting.
10. **Lift-out hero.** The moment that matters: a control lifts out of its
    dimmed parent, flies to centre, scales up with a glow, and is pressed there.
11. **Collapse to a mark.** Complex compositions resolve into a single circle
    with a ✓ — a full stop at the end of an act.
12. **Pacing.** One readable beat every 1.6–2.2s. Type-only acts breathe; proof
    acts are denser. The film never holds a static frame.

### 1.3 Typography

Geometric grotesque, medium to semibold, tight tracking on the display sizes,
sentence case. One idea per frame. Big lines are cropped by the frame edges
rather than shrunk to fit. Keyword words are gradient-filled; the rest is white,
ink, or a muted grey.

### 1.4 Colour, light and sound

- Black stage `#08070C → #16101F`; violet `#7B2FF7` and pink `#FF2D8A` crescents.
- White stage `#FAF8FC`; pink `#FF5FA2`, violet `#8B5CF6`, blue `#5B8DEF` washes.
- Accents are pink→violet linear gradients on keywords, buttons, and the mark.
- UI fragments are pure white with 20–28px radii, thin violet borders when active.
- Sound is music-led with typewriter ticks, soft clicks on presses, whooshes on
  card flights, a rising swell into each inversion, and chimes on the ✓ collapses.

### 1.5 Why it works

The film never lists features. It makes a claim in typography, proves it with
*one isolated piece of real UI*, punctuates with an inversion, and moves on
before the interface gets dense. Because it shows fragments rather than screens,
it can cover nine product areas in 98 seconds without ever asking the viewer to
scan a full application. The stage — dark or light, always lit by drifting
blooms — is the continuity that makes nine unrelated screens feel like one
product world.
