# Reference evidence: Introducing Canvas in Gemini

Original document: mori-promo/CREATIVE_DIRECTION.md

Reference: Google, "Introducing Canvas in Gemini"

Source: https://youtu.be/4Leardp_AGc

The reference is a 60-second, 16:9, 30 fps product film. It uses no explanatory
voiceover. Music, interface sounds, and typography carry the story. The visual
system is a near-black stage with blue and violet edge light, short value phrases,
shallow 3D interface slabs, and cursor-led proof.

### Macro structure

| Time | Movement | Construction and purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0-1.6 | Brand prelude | Gemini's mark and wordmark sit alone on black. A low blue-violet horizon glow gives the frame depth before any UI appears. |
| 1.6-7.0 | Product world | A dark Gemini workspace floats forward as a large perspective slab. The greeting remains legible while the panel rotates through edge-on views, establishing the interface as a physical object. |
| 7.0-10.9 | Promise assembly | "Your AI assistant from Google" fragments and reassembles. Letters disappear behind a moving light flare, then settle into a clean line. The transition turns typography into a reveal mechanism. |
| 10.9-15.7 | Product introduction | "Introducing" resolves to "Canvas in Gemini." A rounded Canvas control grows from the dark stage with an electric edge and tiny particles. The product name receives more screen time than the setup. |
| 15.7-21.9 | First workflow | "From the first..." leads into a prompt pill and a writing canvas. The camera pushes into the prompt, then the resulting document fills the frame. Value text comes first, product proof second. |
| 21.9-31.2 | Editing proof | The camera crops tightly into selected text and compact editing controls. A hand cursor presses real UI. Suggestions appear as luminous floating cards while the document remains visible behind them. |
| 31.2-39.1 | Code transformation | "Turn your concepts into code" is split across successive beats. A prompt becomes code, then a Code/Preview control is clicked. The click supplies a clear cause-and-effect moment. |
| 39.1-47.0 | Breadth montage | Interactive prototypes, documents, timelines, and data examples glide past as layered slabs. The edit accelerates, but each beat still has one central object. |
| 47.0-51.7 | Delight beat | Pixel-art game elements burst into the scene, adding a playful reward after the functional proof. Motion briefly becomes more energetic and spatial. |
| 51.7-60.0 | Brand close | The montage collapses back into the product icon, a final Gemini workspace, and "Try Canvas in Gemini today." Motion slows and the logo receives a clean hold. |

### Motion language

- The edit alternates words and things: a short promise appears, then the real UI
  proves it.
- Interface screens are treated as thin physical slabs using perspective, Y-axis
  turns, push-ins, and soft edge light. There is no literal 3D environment.
- Most entrances combine opacity, scale, and position. Objects settle with a soft
  spring rather than a linear stop.
- Tight crops are purposeful. They isolate the exact control being discussed and
  keep the viewer from scanning an entire interface at once.
- Cursor presses are rare and meaningful. Every click changes visible state.
- Transitions use a moving bloom, an edge-on panel, or a saturated light smear to
  hide the cut.
- The pacing averages one readable beat every 1.5 to 2.5 seconds, then accelerates
  during the breadth montage and exhales on the brand lockup.

### Typography and hierarchy

- Clean geometric sans, medium weight, centered or optically balanced.
- Most frames contain one short sentence. One keyword is blue or violet while the
  rest remains white.
- Text often assembles in clusters rather than simply fading in. Missing letter
  groups and moving flares create anticipation without compromising the final read.
- Product names and verbs receive the strongest color treatment.

### Color, light, and sound

- Background: almost-black with blue-violet horizon light.
- Product slabs: charcoal surfaces with electric blue and violet borders.
- Copy: white with selective blue-violet keywords.
- Lighting is concentrated behind the focal object, never spread evenly across the
  whole frame.
- The soundtrack is music-led and nonverbal. Clicks, whooshes, typing pulses, and
  small chimes make visible interactions feel physical.

### Why the reference works

The film does not narrate features in a list. It makes each promise, proves it with
one real interaction, and moves on before the interface becomes visually dense. The
dark stage creates continuity across many different screens, while consistent edge
light and camera perspective make every UI element feel part of one product world.
